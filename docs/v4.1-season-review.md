# OPTICAL WEATHER v4.1 — season review of programmes 37–43

## Scope

This review began by treating programmes 37–42 as a six-programme season rather than six isolated additions. It tested curatorial coherence, duplication, geographic and maker balance, source resilience, accessibility/context, duration and fit with the v4.1 content architecture. The review itself then commissioned programme 43 to correct the clearest structural gap it identified.

## Season verdict

Programmes 37–43 are strong enough to retain. Each makes a distinct argument and none should be cut simply to reduce catalogue size.

- **37 — Drawn on Film: Ink / Paper / Light**: material cinema, direct animation, optical sound and handmade image-making.
- **38 — Instructions for Living**: public-information film as behavioural technology rather than industrial-film nostalgia.
- **39 — Mexico: Cinema Rescued**: preservation, loss and political counter-memory rather than a national-cinema sampler.
- **40 — Korea Before the Wave**: postwar modernity, class, gender and censorship before contemporary global K-cinema branding.
- **41 — Japan Before Anime**: pre-1945 animation ecology, including education, technical development and militarisation.
- **42 — The Camera Finds the Street**: actuality, urban observation, traffic/planning and the street becoming conscious of the camera.
- **43 — The Camera Turns Back: Australian Feminist Counter-Images**: feminist collective filmmaking, self-representation, labour, family archive, First Nations authorship and the camera as a contested apparatus.

Programmes 37–42 add roughly 37.5 hours of authored viewing; programme 43 adds a further 6h 35m without materially duplicating the historical v3 programme set.

## Findings and actions

### 1. Source geography is part of access

A verified institutional source is not necessarily globally playable. BFI Player states that its playback service is available only in the UK. v4.1 therefore keeps BFI as the authoritative archival source but labels BFI Player links **UK only** and supplies a resilient `Find current copy` route when an item has no editorial fallback.

This is a general rule for future curation: source authority and viewer availability are separate claims.

### 2. Maker balance required a commissioning-level correction

The initial 37–42 season was geographically broader than v3 but overwhelmingly male-authored. This was partly historical to the selected institutions and periods, but the imbalance was too large to treat as incidental.

The review rejected tokenistic substitutions inside historically coherent programmes 39–41 and instead commissioned programme 43 around Australian feminist and women’s experimental moving-image practice.

Programme 43 now brings Sue Ford, the Sydney Women’s Film Group, Robin Laurie, Margot Nash, Jeni Thornley, Essie Coffey, Merilee Bennett, Tracey Moffatt and related collaborators into the authored programme layer. It does not eliminate the collection-wide imbalance, but it materially changes the season’s perspective rather than cosmetically changing credits.

### 3. Geographic balance remains incomplete

The new season adds Mexico, Korea, Japan, Aotearoa New Zealand and a substantial Australian programme to a collection historically weighted toward Europe/North America.

Programme 43 also begins to address the absence of First Nations authorship through Essie Coffey and Tracey Moffatt, but this should not be mistaken for a comprehensive Indigenous moving-image programme.

Major absences remain: Africa, South Asia beyond reservoir material, the Middle East, Indigenous moving-image traditions at programme scale, and much of Latin America outside Mexico. These should shape later commissions rather than being forced into the current season.

### 4. Six-hour duration remains useful but is not a dogma

The six-hour scale works for OPTICAL WEATHER's all-night / screen-in-a-room use case, but future programmes may be shorter when the argument is stronger at three or four hours. Padding to hit a duration target is a curatorial failure.

### 5. Collection blocks are legitimate catalogue objects

Programme 42 uses institutional collection blocks for early actuality rather than pretending dozens of minute-long films are a single work. This convention is retained.

A collection block counts as one catalogue route even when the external destination contains many films. The public count therefore measures OPTICAL WEATHER routes, not the number of individual external works behind every route.

### 6. Context is not optional for propaganda or harmful historical material

Programmes 38 and 41 flag distressing public-safety imagery, historical medical instruction, militarism, propaganda and racist enemy caricature. Programme 43 carries First Nations deceased-person context and flags sexual/nude material where relevant.

Such notes should remain concise, factual and visible before the user leaves OPTICAL WEATHER.

### 7. Architecture is working; programme 37 modularisation is deferred deliberately

Programmes 38–43 live in separate editorial modules loaded after `curation.js` and before the registry. Programme 37 remains embedded in `curation.js`.

The review considered extracting 37 immediately, but this would be a pure structural change to a large, already-tested programme with no public benefit. It is therefore deferred until the next maintenance-only pass rather than mixed into the current curatorial review. This is technical debt, not a release blocker.

## Programme 43 outcome

**43 — The Camera Turns Back: Australian Feminist Counter-Images**

The commission is complete at 12 works / 395 minutes. Its argument is not “women in film”; it is makers taking control of the apparatus and turning the camera back on identity, labour, domesticity, sexuality, colonial history and inherited family/national archives.

Institutional anchors include ACMI, NFSA research, and SBS On Demand. Direct institutional viewing is used where full works are available; otherwise resilient searches are retained rather than pretending an archival information page is a full watch source.

## Next commissioning priority

**44 — Signal / Feedback / Video Machine** remains a strong candidate, but it no longer has automatic priority simply because it was previously named.

Before commissioning 44, compare at least three gaps:

1. analogue video / synthesis / feedback / electronic image-making;
2. Indigenous moving-image practice at programme scale;
3. Africa / South Asia / Middle East archival or experimental cinema.

The next number should go to the strongest curatorial argument with sufficiently good current viewing routes, not to the idea that was written down first.
