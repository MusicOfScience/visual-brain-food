# OPTICAL WEATHER v4.1 — season review of programmes 37–42

## Scope

This review treats programmes 37–42 as a six-programme season rather than six isolated additions. It tests curatorial coherence, duplication, geographic and maker balance, source resilience, accessibility/context, duration and fit with the v4.1 content architecture.

## Season verdict

Programmes 37–42 are strong enough to retain. Each makes a distinct argument and none should be cut simply to reduce catalogue size.

- **37 — Drawn on Film: Ink / Paper / Light**: material cinema, direct animation, optical sound and handmade image-making.
- **38 — Instructions for Living**: public-information film as behavioural technology rather than industrial-film nostalgia.
- **39 — Mexico: Cinema Rescued**: preservation, loss and political counter-memory rather than a national-cinema sampler.
- **40 — Korea Before the Wave**: postwar modernity, class, gender and censorship before contemporary global K-cinema branding.
- **41 — Japan Before Anime**: pre-1945 animation ecology, including education, technical development and militarisation.
- **42 — The Camera Finds the Street**: actuality, urban observation, traffic/planning and the street becoming conscious of the camera.

The six programmes together add roughly 37.5 hours of authored viewing without materially duplicating the historical v3 programme set.

## Findings and actions

### 1. Source geography is part of access

A verified institutional source is not necessarily globally playable. BFI Player states that its playback service is available only in the UK. v4.1 therefore keeps BFI as the authoritative archival source but labels BFI Player links **UK only** and supplies a resilient `Find current copy` route when an item has no editorial fallback.

This is a general rule for future curation: source authority and viewer availability are separate claims.

### 2. Maker balance is the largest curatorial weakness

The season is geographically broader than v3 but remains overwhelmingly male-authored. This is partly historical to the selected institutions and periods, but the imbalance is too large to treat as incidental.

Do not tokenistically alter historically coherent programmes 39–41 merely to rebalance credits. Correct the collection at commissioning level.

The next programme should therefore address women’s and feminist experimental moving-image practice directly. Australian material is unusually strong and institutionally accessible through ACMI and NFSA, including Sue Ford, the Sydney Women’s Film Group, Reel Women, Margot Nash, Robin Laurie, Jeni Thornley, Martha Ansara and related collectives.

### 3. Geographic balance remains incomplete

The new season adds Mexico, Korea, Japan and Aotearoa New Zealand to a collection historically weighted toward Europe/North America, but major absences remain: Africa, South Asia beyond BFI reservoir material, the Middle East, Indigenous moving-image traditions and much of Latin America outside Mexico.

These should shape later commissions rather than being forced into the current six.

### 4. Six-hour duration remains useful but is not a dogma

The six-hour scale works for OPTICAL WEATHER's all-night / screen-in-a-room use case, but future programmes may be shorter when the argument is stronger at three or four hours. Padding to hit a duration target is a curatorial failure.

### 5. Collection blocks are legitimate catalogue objects

Programme 42 uses institutional collection blocks for early actuality rather than pretending dozens of minute-long films are a single work. This convention is retained.

A collection block counts as one catalogue route even when the external destination contains many films. The public count therefore measures OPTICAL WEATHER routes, not the number of individual external works behind every route.

### 6. Context is not optional for propaganda or harmful historical material

Programmes 38 and 41 correctly flag distressing public-safety imagery, historical medical instruction, militarism, propaganda and racist enemy caricature. Such notes should remain concise, factual and visible before the user leaves OPTICAL WEATHER.

### 7. Architecture is working, with one cleanup still desirable

Programmes 38–42 now live in separate editorial modules loaded after `curation.js` and before the registry. Programme 37 remains embedded in `curation.js`; this is not a release blocker, but modularising 37 is desirable before v4.1 is finally merged so that the human-editable reservoir file does not become a monolith.

## Next commission

**43 — working title: The Camera Turns Back**

Commission Australian feminist and women’s experimental moving image around self-representation, the body, labour, domestic space, time, photographic seeing and collective film practice.

Priority institutional research:

- ACMI — Sue Ford Moving Image Collection and individual digitised works;
- NFSA — Feminist Films collection and Sydney Women’s Film Group material;
- Margot Nash / Robin Laurie — *We Aim to Please*;
- Jeni Thornley and collaborators — *A Film for Discussion*, *Maidens* and related work;
- Reel Women / Victorian Women’s Film Unit;
- adjacent Australian experimental artists where full or resilient viewing routes can be verified.

The commission must not become a generic “women in film” survey. Its argument should be about makers taking control of the apparatus and turning the camera back on identity, labour, domesticity, sexuality and the image of women itself.

## Deferred commission

**Signal / Feedback / Video Machine** remains a strong candidate for 44 or later. It should be developed after programme 43 rather than used to postpone the more obvious representational gap identified by this review.
