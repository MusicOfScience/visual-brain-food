## Governing access rule

For new commissioned programmes, prefer the latest authoritative version that is also broadly accessible. Keep authority and playback as separate questions.

Priority order:

1. latest restoration/revision + legitimate globally accessible full playback;
2. latest authoritative restoration/revision metadata + another legitimate full playback route, with the version difference stated;
3. authoritative catalogue/restoration source + resilient `Find current copy` discovery;
4. geofenced, paid, preview-only or research-login sources remain provenance/context unless no better route exists and the limitation is explicit.

Never describe a catalogue page, preview or expired streaming window as a full watch source.

## 45 — Who Holds the Camera? First Nations Moving Image

Status: **high editorial priority; source-build, not yet commissionable**.

NFSA currently states that it holds more than 30,000 audiovisual items representing First Nations experiences. Its curated First Nations short-film route identifies Wayne Blair, Beck Cole, Ivan Sen, Catriona McKenzie, Warwick Thornton and major development series including *From Sand to Celluloid*, *Shifting Sands*, *Crossing Tracks*, *On Wheels*, *Dreaming in Motion* and *Dramatically Black*.

Constraint: the current free NFSA curated pages frequently expose previews rather than complete films, while catalogue access does not imply personal online viewing. NFSA is therefore authority/context unless an individual item explicitly exposes complete public playback.

Commissioning requirement: build the running order from legitimate full works available through filmmakers, Indigenous-controlled broadcasters/platforms, museums or other current rights-holders; use NFSA to verify history, version and cultural context.

Cultural requirement: retain deceased-person warnings and Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property considerations where relevant; do not treat access friction as a technical inconvenience to route around.

## 46 — Nile / Desert / Revolution: Arab and African Counter-Archives

Status: **strong corpus; public-playback gap remains**.

### Sudanese Film Group

Arsenal digitally restored seven works by Sudanese Film Group filmmakers in 2018. Current authoritative records include films by Ibrahim Shaddad, Eltayeb Mahdi and Suliman Elnour; Arsenal's published SFG edition provides English/Arabic subtitles and a 174-minute restored programme.

Current constraint: Arsenal's online viewing service is password protected and explicitly for research purposes. Treat Arsenal pages as restoration/provenance records, not public `Watch` links.

### Atteyat Al Abnoudy / Cimatheque

Cimatheque digitised films and archival material from Atteyat Al Abnoudy's holdings. Arsenal's Forum Expanded 2026 record for *Sad Song of Touha* identifies a new digital restoration carried out in 2025 under Cimatheque's auspices.

This makes the 2025 restoration the preferred version metadata for future programming. It does **not** create a public full-stream route by itself.

Commissioning requirement: locate legitimate current full playback for enough restored Al Abnoudy/SFG works to support a coherent six-hour programme without relying on expired Arsenal 3 windows or research credentials.

## 47 — Cinema Restored Against Forgetting: India

Status: **current restoration story is strong; distribution gate remains**.

Film Heritage Foundation currently foregrounds:

- the restoration of Pradip Krishen and Arundhati Roy's *In Which Annie Gives It Those Ones* (1989), premiered at Berlinale 2026.

These restoration records should control version metadata when the films enter OPTICAL WEATHER. Do not substitute an older circulating upload and imply it is the restored master.
