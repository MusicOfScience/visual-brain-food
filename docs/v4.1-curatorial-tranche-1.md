# OPTICAL WEATHER v4.1 — curatorial tranche 1

## Principle

Expansion is not a request for more links. Each new programme must make an argument the historical 36 do not already make, and each direct source must be defendable at copy/source level.

The first tranche deliberately widens the collection across institutional archives, artists' moving image, non-US/European film histories, material processes and quiet screen-as-room viewing.

## Programme 37 — Drawn on Film: Ink / Paper / Light

**Status: commissioned in `content/curation.js`.**

A six-hour argument about cinema as physical material: direct drawing, painting and scratching on film; patterned-paper cutout; optical sound; electronic line; and reflective documentary on how those marks were made.

Primary source spines:

- National Film Board of Canada — Norman McLaren / Evelyn Lambart
- Govett-Brewster Art Gallery / Len Lye Centre, with films supplied by Ngā Taonga Sound & Vision
- National Film Archive of Japan — Noburō Ōfuji / Japanese Animated Film Classics

The sequence is designed as **mark → colour → synthetic sound → scratched darkness → paper → process**, not as a survey of 'experimental animation'. Archival incompleteness or missing historical sound is stated at item level where verified.

## Programme 38 — Instructions for Living

**Status: research / commissioning.**

Not a second industrial-film programme. Programme 16 already owns factories, machines and labour choreography.

Programme 38 asks how moving images instruct people to become legible social subjects: how to wash, cough, cross a road, store food, parent, work, nurse, smoke or not smoke, respond to danger, consume, behave in public, and inhabit the state's idea of citizenship.

Research spines:

- BFI Player — COI at 75: https://player.bfi.org.uk/free/collection/coi-at-75
- BFI Player — British Propaganda Film: https://player.bfi.org.uk/free/collection/british-propaganda-film
- Library of Congress — National Screening Room: https://www.loc.gov/collections/national-screening-room/
- NFSA online collection: https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection
- National Film Board of Canada: https://www.nfb.ca/

Promising BFI works include *Coughs and Sneezes*, *Handkerchief Drill*, *The Good Housewife "In Her Kitchen"*, *The Smoking Machine*, *Smoking and You*, *Your Children's Play*, *Life in Her Hands*, *Never Go with Strangers*, *Lonely Water*, *The Fatal Floor*, *Insaaf* and *Your Very Good Health*.

The programme should move from apparently trivial bodily instruction toward increasingly explicit questions of public health, danger, work, family, race/community address and state persuasion. It should retain humour where the films are funny without treating their disciplinary function as kitsch.

## Programme 39 — Mexico: Cinema Rescued

**Status: research.**

Use Filmoteca UNAM's free Cine en línea collection as the archival spine. The argument is preservation, memory and the recovered image, rather than a generic national-cinema sampler.

Source: https://cineenlinea.filmoteca.unam.mx/

## Programme 40 — Korea Before the Wave

**Status: research.**

Use Korean Film Archive / Korean Classic Film to move through post-war modernity, gender, class, urbanisation and political rupture before contemporary global Korean cinema became the default frame.

Source: https://www.youtube.com/@KoreanFilm

## Programme 41 — Japan Before Anime

**Status: research.**

Use the National Film Archive of Japan's 64-film Japanese Animated Film Classics collection to examine cutout, silhouette, drawing, cel, folklore, propaganda and small-scale independent production before the industrial anime era.

Source: https://animation.filmarchives.jp/en/index.html

Programme 37 deliberately uses only a focused Ōfuji strand; 41 should remain broad enough to include other pioneers and techniques rather than duplicating it.

## Programme 42 — The Camera Finds the Street

**Status: research.**

Actuality, transport, shopfronts, crowds, anonymous people, work, leisure and changing cities. Prioritise archival films in which the incidental social field becomes the point of watching now.

Research spines:

- EYE silent film: https://www.eyefilm.nl/en/collection/collections/film/silent-film
- Library of Congress National Screening Room: https://www.loc.gov/collections/national-screening-room/
- BFI Player free archive: https://player.bfi.org.uk/free/collections
- NFSA online collection: https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection

## Later candidates

### 43 — Signal / Feedback / Video Machine

Analogue video synthesis, feedback, broadcast technology and electronic image processing. LUX and ICA/Experimental Television Center research should anchor the curatorial history; viewing routes must be checked work by work.

### 44 — Australian Private Worlds

Sue Ford, amateur film, home movies and vernacular image-making. ACMI and NFSA are research anchors. Avoid turning this into a generic Australian film survey.

### Screen as Gallery II

Only commission if research produces a genuinely new premise beyond historical Programmes 21 and 22. A new number is not justified by finding more virtual museum tours.

## Release rule

A programme enters `content/curation.js` only when:

1. its argument is distinct from the historical catalogue;
2. its running order reaches the intended long-form session without padding;
3. title, maker/year and runtime have been checked;
4. every item has either an authoritative current watch source or a precise resilient search route;
5. institutional sources carry source provenance and verification date;
6. known archival incompleteness, missing sound, translation or contextual issues are stated rather than inferred away;
7. sensory/access claims are made only when checked;
8. CI passes after insertion.
